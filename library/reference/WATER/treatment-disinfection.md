# Water — Emergency Treatment & Disinfection

> Category: **WATER** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary source: **CDC** — *Making Water Safe in an Emergency* (verified 2026-09-06)
> **Not a substitute for professional guidance.** Confirm current local guidance before relying.

If you don't have safe bottled water, make water safe with **one** of these methods. The
best method depends on whether the water is **clear** or **cloudy**, and what supplies you
have.

---

## 1. Boiling (best method — kills most germs)

1. **If the water is cloudy**, first filter it through a clean cloth, paper towel, or coffee
   filter to remove particles.
2. Bring water to a **rolling boil** for **1 minute**.
3. **At elevations above 6,500 feet (≈2,000 m)**, boil for **3 minutes** (water boils at a
   lower temperature up high, so it needs longer).
4. Let it cool. Store cooled water in **clean, sanitised containers with tight covers**.

Improve the flat taste of boiled water:

- Pour it from one container to another and let it stand a few hours, and/or
- Add a pinch of salt per quart/litre.

> **Limitation:** Boiling does **not** reliably kill the parasites *Cryptosporidium* and
> *Giardia*. For water you suspect contains these, chemical disinfection (below) is more
> effective. Boil **and** chemically treat when both are needed.

---

## 2. Chemical disinfection with unscented chlorine bleach

Use **non-perfumed household bleach** that contains **sodium hypochlorite**. Read the label
for concentration. If the water is cloudy, **filter first** (clean cloth/paper towel/coffee
filter).

### 2.1 Bleach at 5%–9% sodium hypochlorite (most common household bleach)

| Water amount | Dropper | Millilitres (mL) | Measuring spoon |
|--------------|---------|------------------|-----------------|
| **1 gallon / 3.8 L** | **8 drops** | ½ mL | a little less than ⅛ tsp |
| **5 gallons / 19 L** | **40 drops** | 2½ mL | ½ tsp |

### 2.2 Bleach at 1% sodium hypochlorite (less-concentrated bleach)

| Water amount | Dropper | Millilitres (mL) | Measuring spoon |
|--------------|---------|------------------|-----------------|
| **1 gallon / 3.8 L** | **40 drops** | 2½ mL | ⅛ tsp |
| **5 gallons / 19 L** | **200 drops** | 12½ mL | 2½ tsp |

### Steps
1. Stir in the bleach.
2. **Wait 30 minutes** before drinking.
3. If the water still tastes/smells of chlorine, let it stand longer.

> **Check your bleach %:** "5%–9%" is the typical modern household concentration. If your
> bleach label says a different %, use the table matching THAT concentration. Never use
> scented, splash-less, or additively bleaches.

---

## 3. Chemical tablets or drops

Chlorine dioxide, chlorine, or iodine tablets/drops kill most germs and are very portable.
**Follow the package label exactly** for dose and contact time.

> **Limitation:** iodine is **not** for long-term use, and should be avoided by pregnant
> women and people with thyroid conditions. Chlorine dioxide also handles *Cryptosporidium*
> better than plain chlorine/iodine for extended filtering.

---

## 4. Filters

Use a **certified microfilter** (pore size **≤ 1 micron**, ideally 0.1 µm) for
*Cryptosporidium* and *Giardia*. A filter alone does NOT remove viruses — pair filtration
with chemical disinfection or boiling when viral contamination is possible.

---

## Do NOT use
- **Boilers that are part of a home-heating system** for drinking, cooking, or hygiene.
- Water that has a strong chemical/odour, contains fuel/chemicals, or is visibly
  contaminated beyond simple cloudiness — treat or discard per guidance.
