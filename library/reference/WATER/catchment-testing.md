# Water — Catchment & Testing

> Category: **WATER** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> General guidance + the operational "is it safe?" method. Treat all non-bottled water.

## 1. Catchment — collecting rainwater & runoff

Precise systems are region/hardware dependent; keep the essentials:
- **Catchment surface** that is as clean as possible (roof/gutters). A **first-flush
  diverter** (discarding the first runoff, which carries dust/bird droppings/roof debris)
  dramatically improves water: at minimum, let the first part of any storm run off before
  collecting.
- **Gutter guards / filters** at the collection point to keep out leaves and debris.
- Store collected water in dark, covered, food-grade containers to prevent algae and
  mosquito breeding. **Screen all inlets** (fine mesh) against mosquitoes.
- Collected rainwater is **NOT safe to drink untreated** — it needs filtration +
  disinfection (see `treatment-disinfection.md`) before drinking.

## 2. Emergency ground sources

Wells, springs, and running streams are generally better than still/standing water. When
collecting from an unproven or emergency source:

- Take from **flowing** water and **upstream of** any animals/structures/settlements.
- **Avoid** water near sewage outflow, stagnant pools, industrial sites, or agricultural
  runoff.
- Collect into clean containers, then **treat** before drinking (boil or disinfect).

## 3. Testing — the practical "is it safe?" layer

You cannot reliably judge safety by look, taste, or smell. The realistic pre-drinking
method is:

1. **Clear vs cloudy** — if cloudy/murky, filter first (clean cloth/paper/coffee filter).
2. **Boil** (1 min, 3 min above 6,500 ft) OR **chemically disinfect** (bleach/tablets).
   This handles microbial pathogens — the main acute risk.
3. **If you have test strips/a kit:** check for the obvious chemical hazards you can (e.g. a
   basic test can indicate extreme metal/chemical issues), but understand a field kit does
   **not** prove water free of all chemical contaminants.

**Hard truth of emergency testing:** in the field, boiling+disinfection is the standard that
protects against the acute microbiological threat. Full laboratory testing for chemical
contamination is only possible when normal lab services are available. Do not let absence of
lab testing stop you treating water for microbes.

## 4. Red flags that should make you NOT drink a source
- Visible contamination / oils / sheen / strong chemical or fuel odour.
- Known or suspected sewage or industrial contamination nearby.
- Water with dead animals or heavy algae growth that you cannot clear.
