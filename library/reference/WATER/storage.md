# Water — Storage & Stockpiling

> Category: **WATER** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> General guidance from US federal emergency-preparedness practice (FEMA/Ready.gov,
> CDC). Not a substitute for professional advice.

## 1. How much to store

The standard emergency-preparedness target is **1 gallon (≈3.8 L) of water per person per
day** for drinking and sanitation, plus a bit more in hot climates / for pregnant people /
the medically vulnerable / heavy physical activity.

Plan for **at least a 2-week supply** for resilience planning:

- **2 weeks** ≈ **14 gallons (≈53 L) per person.**

For a household of 4, a 2-week supply ≈ **56 gallons (≈210 L).**

> Store more than this minimum if you can — it is used for cooking, hygiene, and pets, and
> demand rises fast in an outage.

## 2. Containers

- Use **food-grade** containers (marked "food safe" / "food-grade", or clean 2-3L soda
  bottles; commercial bottled water is the easiest).
- **Never** reuse containers that held milk, juice, or chemicals — residues breed bacteria.
- **Never** use containers previously used to hold any toxic or non-food material.
- Clean and disinfect before filling (wash then sanitise with a dilute bleach solution,
  then rinse).

## 3. Filling, sealing, rotating

- Fill to the top to displace air, cap tightly, and store away from light and heat.
- Label each container with the **fill date**.
- Commercially bottled water: keep the **original seal**; check and replace when the
  printed "best by / expiry" date passes (typically 1–2 years).
- Self-fill household containers: **rotate every 6 months** — empty, re-sanitise, refill.
- Inspect storage periodically; discard cloudy, leaking, or off-smelling water.

## 4. Where to store

- **Cool, dark** place (warmer temps and light promote algae and degrade containers).
- Off the floor; away from chemical fumes, gas, paint, and stored fuels (vapours can
  permeate some plastics).

## 5. Other emergency water sources (if the stored supply runs short)

- Water from your **hot-water tank** (turn off the unit's power/gas first; drain from the
  bottom, away from the sediment).
- Water from **toilet tanks** (NOT the bowl) — not ideal for drinking, but usable for
  hygiene if it is clear and hasn't been treated with chemical tank additives.
- **Liquid from canned fruit/vegetables** in a pinch.
- **Melting ice** from the freezer — treat it like any non-bottled water.

> Treat **any** source that is not sealed commercial bottled water as needing
> **treatment** (boil or disinfect — see `treatment-disinfection.md`) before drinking.
