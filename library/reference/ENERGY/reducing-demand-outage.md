# Energy & Power — Reducing Demand in an Outage

> Category: **ENERGY** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary source: **Ready.gov — Power Outages** (US DHS/FEMA). Verified 2026-09-08 (Wayback replay).
> **Not a substitute for your utility's instructions.** These are the universal demand-management
> and food-safety steps for when the power is off.

---

## 1. Protect equipment from surges

- **Turn off or disconnect appliances and equipment** in case of a *momentary power "surge"* that
  can damage computers and other devices.
- **Consider adding surge protectors.**

---

## 2. Food safety while the power is out

- **Keep refrigerator and freezer doors closed.**
- *"An unopened refrigerator will keep food cold for about **4 hours**. A full freezer will keep
  the temperature for about **48 hours**."*
- **Throw away any food** exposed to **40 °F (4 °C) for 2 hours or more**, or with an unusual
  odour/colour/texture — *"When in doubt, throw it out!"*
- **If food in the freezer is still colder than 40 °F and has ice crystals, you can refreeze it.**

---

## 3. Fuels, lighting, and staying charged

- **Keep your car's gas tank full** — *gas stations rely on electricity to power their pumps* (a
  full tank is both mobility and a fuel reserve).
- **Only use flashlights** for emergency lighting — *candles can cause fires.*
- **Prepare:** keep a kit with flashlight, batteries, **cash**, and first-aid supplies; have
  **alternative charging methods** for phones/devices; **charge cell phones and battery devices**
  before / early in the outage.

---

## 4. The demand-management idea

- Reduce load by **disconnecting non-essential devices** and keeping fridges/freezers **closed**
  (don't open to "check"). This also **protects against surge damage when power returns.**
- For generator/safety specifics, see the ENERGY generator-CO doc — this page deliberately does
  not repeat it.
