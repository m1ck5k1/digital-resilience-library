# Energy & Power — Safe Fuel & Hazardous-Chemical Handling

> Category: **ENERGY** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary source: **US EPA — Household Hazardous Waste (HHW)**. Verified 2026-09-08.
> **Not a substitute for local disposal rules / the product label.** Universal handling and
> disposal principles — no site- or product-specific numbers invented.

---

## 1. What counts as household hazardous waste (EPA)

*"EPA considers some leftover household products that can catch fire, react, or explode under
certain circumstances, or that are corrosive or toxic as household hazardous waste. Products such
as **paints, cleaners, oils, batteries, and pesticides** can contain hazardous ingredients and
require special care when you dispose of them."* — this includes many fuels, oils, and chemicals a
household stores for backup power or maintenance.

---

## 2. Safe handling & storage rules (EPA)

- **Never store hazardous products in food containers** — *keep them in their original containers
  and never remove labels.*
- **Follow label instructions** for use and storage *"to prevent any accidents at home."*
- **Read label disposal directions** to reduce risk of products *"exploding, igniting, leaking,
  mixing with other chemicals, or posing other hazards."*
- **Never mix leftover HHW with other products** — *"incompatible products might react, ignite, or
  explode, and contaminated HHW might become unrecyclable."*
- **Corroding containers need special handling** — call your local hazardous-materials official or
  fire department for instructions.

---

## 3. Why disposal matters

- **Improper disposal** (pouring down drains, on the ground, into storm sewers, or in regular
  trash) *"can pollute the environment and pose a threat to human health"*, and *"can contaminate
  septic tanks or wastewater treatment systems."* They can also present hazards to **children and
  pets** if left around the house.
- **Even empty containers** can pose hazards because of residual chemicals.

---

## 4. Disposing safely

- **Check with your local environmental, health, or solid-waste agency** for collection options.
- Some facilities (e.g. **local garages**) may accept specific items like **used motor oil** for
  recycling.
