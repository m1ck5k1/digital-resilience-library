# Energy — Off-Grid & Backup Power (Generators, Safety, CO)

> Category: **ENERGY** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary sources: **EPA** (CO safety factsheet + infographics), **Ready.gov / FEMA**,
> **CPSC**. Verified live 2026-09-07.
> **Not a substitute for the equipment manufacturer's instructions or local codes.**

## What this covers

Portable generator use for a household power outage, the **carbon-monoxide (CO) hazard**,
generator electrical/fuel safety, and what *not* to do indoors. This doc is deliberately about
**safety** — the fixed truth that keeps a family alive — not appliance sizing (see
"Not covered" at the end).

---

## 1. The core hazard: carbon monoxide (CO)

- CO is an **odorless, colorless, toxic gas** — impossible to see, taste, or smell. You can be
  poisoned before you realise it's present.
- Portable generators are a leading source of accidental **non-fire CO poisoning**:
  **more than 150 people in the US die every year** from such poisoning linked to consumer
  products, including generators.
- **Symptoms** of CO poisoning: headache, dizziness, weakness, **nausea, vomiting**,
  confusion, sleepiness, shortness of breath — flu-like symptoms **without a fever**.
- **If you suspect CO poisoning: get to fresh air immediately, then call for medical help
  (911 / local emergency number).**

> There is **no safe indoor use** for a portable, fuel-fired generator. This is the single
> most important fact in this document.

---

## 2. Where to run a generator (placement)

Rule of thumb: **outdoors and at least 20 feet (≈6 m) from buildings, windows, and doors.**

- **Never** run a generator **inside** the home, garage, **basement, crawlspace, shed**, or
  any enclosed/partly-enclosed area — even with doors and windows open.
- **Never** run it on a **balcony, deck, or porch**, or within **20 ft of a window, door, or
  attached garage**.
- Deadly CO levels can build up quickly in confined/close areas and can **linger for hours,
  even after the generator has shut off**.

---

## 3. CO alarms (detection)

- Install **CO alarms in a central location on every level** of the living space
  (including the basement), and **at least 15 feet away from fuel-burning appliances**.
- Place alarms **near or inside each sleeping area**, and in the home near the door to an
  attached garage (and in rooms over the garage).
- **Test CO alarms once a month**; interconnect them when possible (when one sounds, they all
  sound). Follow the manufacturer's replacement schedule and local building code.
- Use **battery-operated CO alarms, or alarms with battery backup**, so they keep working
  during a power outage.

---

## 4. Generator electrical & operating safety

- **Keep the generator dry** and protected from rain/flooding. **Touching a wet generator —
  or devices connected to one — can cause electrical shock.** Operate it on a dry surface.
- Connect appliances with **heavy-duty extension cords** (rated for the load; don't use thin
  indoor cords outdoors).
- **Let the generator cool before refueling** — fuel spilled on hot engine parts can ignite.
- Follow the **manufacturer's instructions** carefully.
- Store generators **and fuel** outdoors, away from the home.

---

## 5. Never use these indoors either (other combustion sources)

- **Fuel-fired grills, barbecues, hibachis, or camp stoves** — never indoors.
- **Charcoal grills** — never bring one into the house for heating or cooking; **never
  barbecue in the garage** (charcoal produces CO).
- **Gas range or oven** — never use for heating the home.
- **Any vehicle** in a garage — CO can build to deadly levels **even with the garage door
  open**. Remove the vehicle from the garage immediately after starting it.
- Safer indoor options: a **vented** fireplace or a **vented** wood-/other-fuel-burning stove
  (if set up for cooking).
- Lighting: use **flashlights or battery lanterns**; if using candles, keep them ventilated
  and watched (combustion products + fire hazard).
- Have home heating systems (including chimneys and vents) **inspected and serviced annually**
  by a trained technician.

---

## 6. Food safety during an outage (power is off)

- A **refrigerator** keeps food cold for about **4 hours**; a **full freezer** keeps
  temperature for about **48 hours** — keep the doors closed.
- Use coolers with ice if needed; monitor with a thermometer.
- **Throw out food that reaches 40°F (≈4.5°C) or higher**.
- **Disconnect** appliances/electronics to avoid surge damage — power can return with
  momentary surges or spikes.
