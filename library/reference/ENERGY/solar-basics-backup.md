# Energy & Power — Solar Basics for Backup

> Category: **ENERGY** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary source: **US DOE — Solar Photovoltaic Technology Basics**. Verified 2026-09-08.
> **Not a substitute for a qualified installer / your equipment's manual.** This is the universal
> how-it-works baseline for understanding a small solar backup setup.

---

## 1. How a PV system works (DOE)

- *"Photovoltaic (PV) materials and devices convert sunlight into electrical energy."*
- **A single PV device is a cell** — usually small, *"typically producing about 1 or 2 watts of
  power"*, made of semiconductor materials often *"less than the thickness of four human hairs."*
- Cells are *"sandwiched between protective materials such as glass and/or plastic to withstand
  the outdoors for many years."*
- **Cells → modules (panels) → arrays:** *"To boost the power output, cells are connected in
  chains to form larger units known as modules or panels. Modules can be used individually, or
  several can be connected to form arrays."*

---

## 2. Modularity & the rest of the system

- *"Because of this modular structure, PV systems can be built to meet almost any electric power
  need, small or large."* — a small panel for backup is the same technology scaled down.
- A complete system *"also include[s] mounting structures that point panels toward the sun, along
  with the components that take the direct-current (DC) electricity produced by modules and
  convert it to the alternating-current (AC) electricity used to power… home appliances."* — i.e. an
  **inverter** converts panel **DC → house-AC**, plus the panels need mounting + sun-facing.

---

## 3. What this means for a backup setup

- **Panels make DC**; **an inverter turns it into AC** for appliances; some setups store charge in a
  **battery** (see `batteries-portable-power.md`) or feed a power station.
- Sizing is modular: each cell ~1–2 W; a panel is many cells chained; an array is several panels —
  so you can size up or down to the power need.
- Point panels **toward the sun** for best output (seasonal/positioning matters).
