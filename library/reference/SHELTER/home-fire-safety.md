# Shelter & Home — Home Fire Safety

> Category: **SHELTER** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary sources: **FEMA** — *Are You Ready?*; **Ready.gov — Home Fires**. Verified 2026-09-08.
> **Structural/home-fire awareness** (the backcountry firecraft doc lives in LEARN). General guidance,
> not medical care; if injured, call 9-1-1.

---

## 1. Why it matters — fire is fast

- *"A fire can become life-threatening in just **two minutes**; a residence can be engulfed in
  flames in **five minutes**."* *"In less than 30 seconds a small flame can turn into a major fire."*
- Room temperatures can be **100 °F at floor level and rise to 600 °F at eye level**.
- *"**Smoke and toxic gases kill more people than flames do.**"* (FEMA: asphyxiation is the leading
  cause of fire deaths, "exceeding burns by a three-to-one ratio.")

---

## 2. Smoke alarms — placement, testing, replacement

- **Properly working smoke alarms decrease your chances of dying in a fire by half.**
- Install smoke alarms **on every level including the basement**, **outside bedrooms** (ceiling or
  high on the wall, 4–12 in from the ceiling), at the top of open stairways / bottom of enclosed
  stairs, and near (not in) the kitchen.
- **Replace batteries twice a year** (unless 10-year lithium); **test monthly**; **replace the whole
  unit every 10 years** or per manufacturer.
- **Never disable a smoke alarm while cooking.** Accessibility: audible alarms for visual
  impairment; vibrating-pad/flashing-light alarms for hearing impairment.

---

## 3. Fire escape planning

- **Plan two ways out of each room**, practice the plan **twice a year**, and **practise feeling
  your way out in the dark** (with closed eyes).
- Ensure **windows are not stuck/nailed/painted shut** and security gratings/bars have a **fire-safe
  quick-opening feature**; consider escape ladders on upper levels.
- **Stay low** — smoke and heat rise; the air is clearer/cooler near the floor.
- **Teach children not to hide from firefighters**; check all exits if you use a walker/wheelchair;
  clear trash/storage (don't let newspapers/magazines accumulate).

---

## 4. Prevention

- **Sleep with the door closed**; install **A-B-C fire extinguishers** and teach use; consider
  sprinklers; ask the local fire department to inspect your home.
- **Cooking:** stay in the kitchen when frying/grilling/broiling; turn the stove off when leaving;
  wear close-fitting sleeves; position grills **≥10 ft** from siding, deck railings, and eaves.
- **Heating:** keep combustibles **≥3 ft** from heaters; use only the designated fuel; have heating
  units inspected/cleaned yearly; keep a screen in front of the fireplace; store ashes in a metal
  container outside.
- **Electrical:** replace worn/damaged cords; don't run cords under rugs; put 3-prong plugs in
  3-slot outlets only (never force); replace hot switches/flickering lights; don't overload outlets.
- **Smoking & matches:** keep matches/lighters out of reach of children (ideally locked); never
  smoke in bed or when drowsy; douse butts before disposal.
- **Never use a stove range or oven to heat the home** (CO risk); **never use portable generators
  indoors**, and refuel outdoors in a well-ventilated area.

---

## 5. During a fire — response

- **If your clothes catch fire: Stop, Drop, and Roll** — *"running only makes the fire burn
  faster."* If someone can't stop, smother the flames with a blanket/towel; cool a burn with cool
  water 3–5 minutes, cover with a clean dry cloth, get medical help.
- **Check doors before opening:** feel the top of the door, the knob, and the crack with the **back
  of your hand**. Hot → do **not** open; escape via window. If you can't escape, **signal at the
  window** with a light-coloured cloth or flashlight.
- **Cool door → open slowly**, ready to shut it quickly if smoke/fire is present; **crawl low under
  smoke**; **close doors behind you** to slow the spread.
- **Get out and stay out — do not re-enter. Call 9-1-1.** If someone/pet is trapped, tell the
  operator/firefighters where they are.

---

## 6. After a fire

- Contact disaster relief (e.g. Red Cross) for temporary housing/food/meds; **check with the fire
  department that the home is safe to enter**; **do not reconnect utilities yourself**; inventory
  damaged property before discarding anything; keep receipts for fire-loss spending (insurance/tax);
  notify your mortgage company.
