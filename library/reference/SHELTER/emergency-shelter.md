# Shelter & Heat — Emergency & Improvised Shelter

> Category: **SHELTER** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary source: **US Army FM 21-76** (Survival), Ch.5 & Ch.15 — public-domain US Government
> manual. Verified 2026-09-07.
> **Not a substitute for engineered shelter, professional guidance, or a real building in a
> storm.** This is improvised, keep-you-alive knowledge.

In some situations, shelter beats food — and possibly water. A good shelter *"protect[s] you
from the sun, insects, wind, rain, snow, hot or cold temperatures, and enemy observation."*

---

## 1. The #1 rule: keep it SMALL

- **The most common error in making a shelter is making it too large.** It must be big enough
  to protect you, but **small enough to contain your body heat** — especially in cold.
- FM 21-76 is blunt on cold-weather sizing: *"A fatal error in cold weather shelter
  construction is making the shelter so large that it steals body heat rather than saving it.
  Keep shelter space small."*
- A larger shelter also means **more space to heat** and more wind exposure. Size it to you,
  not to comfort.

---

## 2. Site selection — the BLISS doctrine

- **B** — **Blend in** (concealment).
- **L** — **Low silhouette** (hard to see, less wind).
- **I** — **Irregular shape** (less visible / more natural).
- **S** — **Small** (reduces heat loss).
- **S** — **Secluded location**.

When choosing a spot: confirm there's **material for the shelter**, and a **large, level patch
to lie down**. **Avoid** flash-flood areas, avalanche/rockslide zones, and anywhere at/below
the high-water mark.

---

## 3. Poncho lean-to (fast, effective)

Construction from FM 21-76:
- **Poncho + 2–3 m of rope** and **three ~30 cm stakes**; two trees/poles **2–3 m apart**.
- Turn the **back of the lean-to into the wind**; tie the ropes **waist-high**.
- Add **drip sticks (~10 cm)** to keep rain from running down the rope/grommet line.

---

## 4. Snow shelters

- **Snow cave:** needs a snowdrift ~**3 m deep**; arched roof (strength + melt drainage);
  **sleeping platform higher than the entrance**; a small trench between platform and wall;
  roof high enough to **sit up**; **walls and ceiling at least 30 cm thick**; install a
  **ventilation shaft**; block the entrance with a snow block; use the lower entrance area
  for cooking.
- **Snow trench:** get **below the snow/wind level** to use snow's insulating quality; cut snow
  blocks for overhead cover or use a poncho/material; **only one entrance**, sealed with a snow
  block or rucksack.
- **Others:** snow-block + parachute shelter (clear accumulated snow regularly to avoid
  collapse); igloo/snow house (needs a snow saw/knife + practice); lean-to with snow piled
  around the sides; fallen-tree shelter (dig under a tree, line the floor with branches);
  tree-pit shelter (dig under a large tree, ground sheet overhead).

---

## 5. Cold-shelter safety (non-negotiables)

- **Do not use metal** (e.g. an aircraft fuselage, sheet metal) as shelter in extreme cold —
  it **conducts heat away** from your body.
- **Always block the entrance** to keep heat in and wind out.
- **Ventilate any enclosed shelter — especially if you have a fire.** Stagnant air + combustion
  is how carbon monoxide becomes lethal.
- **CO danger:** *"Never fall asleep without turning out your stove or lamp."* CO is colorless
  and odorless and can cause **unconsciousness or death without warning**. A visible sign is
  **cherry-red lips, mouth, and inside the eyelids**. If suspected, get to fresh air.

---

## 6. Ground insulation

- **Never sleep directly on the ground** — it drains body heat. Lay **pine boughs, grass, or
  other insulating material** underneath you (see `cold-survival-hypothermia.md`).
