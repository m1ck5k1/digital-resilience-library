# Shelter & Home — Storm Protection (Hurricane & Tornado)

> Category: **SHELTER** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary sources: **Ready.gov — Hurricanes** and **Ready.gov — Tornadoes** (US DHS/FEMA).
> Verified 2026-09-08 (Wayback replay).
> **Not a substitute for local officials' instructions.** When told to evacuate, do so — this
> is about protecting your *home and self* when storms threaten.

---

## 1. Hurricanes — before (prepare the property)

- **Know your risk** — hurricanes are not just a coastal problem; rain, wind, water, and even
  tornadoes can occur far inland from landfall.
- **Know your evacuation zone and routes**; practice with the household and pets; identify where
  you'll stay. Follow **local emergency managers** — they issue the latest recommendations.
- **Strengthen the home:** *de-clutter drains and gutters, bring in outside furniture, and
  consider hurricane shutters.*
- **Review documents:** keep insurance policies and personal documents (ID) up to date; make
  copies in a secure, password-protected digital space.
- **Get tech-ready:** keep your cell phone charged when a hurricane is forecast; buy backup
  charging devices.
- **Gather supplies** for several days–weeks: medication, disinfectant, pet supplies, in a go-bag
  or car boot. *"You may not have access to these supplies for days or even weeks after a
  hurricane."*
- **Help neighbours** — check on seniors/those needing extra help.

---

## 2. Hurricanes — during

- **Evacuate immediately** if you're in a mandatory evacuation zone and officials tell you to.
- Protect yourself from high winds: take refuge in a **designated storm shelter or an interior
  room** for high winds.
- **If trapped by flooding:** go to the **highest level** of the building — but **do not climb
  into a closed attic** (you can be trapped by rising water).
- **Never walk, swim, or drive through flood water** — *"Turn Around, Don't Drown. Just six
  inches of fast-moving water can knock you down, and one foot of moving water can sweep your
  vehicle away."*

---

## 3. Tornadoes — before (choose your safe spot)

- **Know the signs:** a rotating funnel-shaped cloud, an approaching cloud of debris, or a loud
  roar "like a freight train."
- **Identify and practice** going to a safe shelter. Best: a **safe room built to FEMA criteria**
  or a **storm shelter built to ICC 500 standards**. Next-best: a **small, interior, windowless
  room or basement on the lowest level** of a sturdy building.
- Get emergency alerts via **EAS, NOAA Weather Radio**, and community sirens (learn the warning
  tone).
- Gather supplies for a longer stay / shelter-in-place: non-perishable food, water, medical
  supplies, medication, cleaning supplies.

---

## 4. Tornadoes — during (when the warning comes)

- **Immediately** go to the safe location you identified. **Stay away from windows, doors, and
  outside walls.** Protect your **head and neck** with your arms; put furniture/blankets around
  or on top of you.
- **In a car:** park as quickly and safely as possible, out of traffic lanes; **stay in the car,
  seat belt on**, head down below the windows, covered with hands + a blanket/coat. If you can
  safely get noticeably lower than the roadway, leave the car and lie there, covering your head.
  **Avoid sheltering under bridges** — they create deadly traffic hazards and offer little
  protection from flying debris.

---

## 5. After a storm (shared)

- **Pay attention to authorities** for information/instructions; return home only when they say
  it's safe.
- **Save phone calls for emergencies** — use **text messages or social media** to communicate
  (phone systems are often down/busy after a disaster).
- **Stay clear of fallen or broken power lines.**
- Wear thick-soled shoes, long pants, and work gloves during cleanup; use face coverings/masks
  when cleaning **mould or debris**. People with asthma/lung conditions or immune suppression
  should not enter buildings with visible/smellable water leaks or mould; **children should not
  help with cleanup**.
- **Document property damage with photographs** for insurance after a hurricane.
