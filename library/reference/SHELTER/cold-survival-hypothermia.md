# Shelter & Heat — Cold Survival & Hypothermia

> Category: **SHELTER** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary sources: **CDC**, **CDC/NIOSH**, **NOAA NWS**, **American Red Cross**, and **US Army
> FM 21-76**. Verified 2026-09-07.
> ⚠ **Not medical advice and not a substitute for professional medical care.** Cold is a quiet
> killer; if you suspect hypothermia or frostbite, get to help / call emergency services.

---

## 1. What hypothermia is & the thresholds

- **Hypothermia** is "the body's failure to maintain a temperature of **36 °C (97 °F)**"
  (FM 21-76) — or, in CDC terms, it occurs when the body **loses heat faster than it can
  produce it**.
- **Crucially, hypothermia can occur at cool temperatures** — *"even at cool temperatures
  (above 40°F) if a person becomes chilled from rain, sweat, or submersion in cold water."*
- **Get medical attention immediately** if a person's temperature is **below 95 °F (35 °C)**.
  A low body temp affects the brain, so a victim *"may not know that it's happening and won't
  be able to do anything about it."* — which is why **bystanders** must act.

---

## 2. Who's at risk / warning signs

**Most at risk (CDC):**
- Older adults with inadequate food, clothing, or heating;
- Babies sleeping in cold bedrooms;
- People outdoors for long periods — the homeless, hikers, hunters;
- People who drink alcohol or use illicit drugs.

**Adult warning signs:** shivering; exhaustion/very tired; confusion; **fumbling hands**;
memory loss; slurred speech; drowsiness.
**Babies:** bright red, cold skin; very low energy.

**Cold-injury types from cold stress (NIOSH):** hypothermia, frostbite, trench foot,
chilblains. Risk rises for anyone outdoors or in poorly insulated/unheated spaces; in regions
unaccustomed to winter, near-freezing temps already count as cold-stress factors.

## 3. First aid for hypothermia (core-warming priorities)

From CDC / Red Cross / FM 21-76 (emergency reference only):

- **Warm the center of the body — chest, neck, head, and groin** — using an electric blanket
  if available, or **skin-to-skin contact under loose, dry layers** of blankets/clothing.
- **Remove all wet clothing first**; get the person into a warm room/shelter.
- **Warm drinks help raise core temperature — but do NOT give alcohol** (and don't give
  anything orally to an unconscious person).
- After the temperature rises, keep the person **dry and wrapped, including head and neck**.
- **Unlike heatstroke, you must warm a hypothermia victim gradually.**
- **Severe hypothermia:** the person may be unconscious and seem not to breathe / have no
  pulse. Handle gently, get emergency help, and **"perform CPR even if the person appears
  dead"**; keep warming during CPR — some who appear dead can be resuscitated.
- In the field (FM 21-76): insulate the victim from the ground, replace wet clothing, improvise
  shelter, give warm liquids/foods, use body heat / warmed items / warm, wrapped objects; let
  an affected person **rest at least 24 hours**.

## 4. Frostbite (a cold injury) — recognition & care

- **Frostbite** is injury from freezing, usually of the **extremities — nose, ears, cheeks,
  chin, fingers, toes**. Severe cases can permanently damage tissue or lead to amputation.
- **Signs:** white or grayish-yellow skin; unusually firm or waxy skin; **numbness**. The
  victim may not notice (the part is numb).
- **Care:** get into a warm room; **do NOT walk** on frostbitten feet/toes; **do NOT rub with
  snow or massage**; warm the area in **warm — not hot — water** (comfortable to the touch), or
  use **body heat** (e.g. armpit for fingers) if no warm water; **do NOT** use heating pads /
  heat lamps / stoves / fireplaces / radiators — numb areas burn easily.
- FM 21-76: light frostbite involves only the skin (dull, whitish); deep frostbite extends
  below the skin with solid, immovable tissue; **do not thaw near an open flame**; gently rub
  in lukewarm water, dry, warm at body temperature. Use a **buddy system** to check exposed
  faces; if alone, cover the nose/lower face.

## 5. Wind chill — the real danger of cold air + wind

- **Wind chill** is the rate of heat loss from exposed skin caused by wind and cold — wind
  draws heat from the body, driving down skin temperature and eventually internal temperature.
- **Worked example (NOAA):** at **0 °F** with **15 mph** wind, wind chill is **−19 °F**, and
  *"at this wind chill temperature, exposed skin can freeze in 30 minutes."*
- Wind chill is defined only for **air temps ≤50 °F and wind >3 mph** (it assumes a clear night
  sky / no sun). Bright **sunshine can raise the wind-chill temp by 10–18 °F**.
- In **extreme cold**, people *"can succumb to hypothermia in a matter of minutes"* (NWS).

## 6. Layering & heat retention (prevention)

- **Wear several layers of loose clothing** for better insulation (NIOSH).
- **Wear a hat** — *"hats reduce body heat escaping from your head."*
- Protect **ears, face, hands, feet**; wear **waterproof, insulated boots**; avoid bare-skin
  contact with cold metal; carry extra socks/gloves/hat/jacket/blankets.
- Red Cross: layers of loose clothing, a coat, hat, mittens, water-resistant boots; a scarf
  over the face and mouth; keep **nose, ears, cheeks, chin, fingers, toes** covered (first at
  risk for frostbite). **Avoid alcohol and caffeine**; drink warm fluids.
- FM 21-76: **never sleep directly on the ground** — lay pine boughs/grass/insulating material
  so the ground doesn't absorb your body heat.
- Stranded in a vehicle (Red Cross): **stay in the vehicle**; wrap in newspapers/maps/removable
  floor mats to trap heat; huddle; run the engine ~**10 min per hour** with the heater, exhaust
  clear, a downwind window slightly open; drink fluids.

---

## 7. Shelter-in-place vs. go to warmth

- Standard advice: **go somewhere warmer than "stay and freeze."** Red Cross: know where you
  will go if your home becomes too cold — a friend's house, a public library, or a **warming
  center**.
- Keep a **Stay-at-Home Kit (≥2 weeks' supplies)** and a **Go-Kit (≥3 days to go somewhere
  warmer)**; **≥1 gallon of drinking water per person per day**.
- **If stranded in a vehicle:** stay and wait — *"do not leave the vehicle to search for
  assistance unless help is visible within 100 yards (91 meters)."* Display a bright (ideally
  red) cloth on the antenna; raise the hood after the snow stops; huddle for warmth.
