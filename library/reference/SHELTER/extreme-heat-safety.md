# Shelter & Home — Extreme Heat Safety

> Category: **SHELTER** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary sources: **CDC**, **NOAA/NWS**, **FEMA** — *Are You Ready?*, **Ready.gov — Extreme Heat**.
> Verified 2026-09-08.
> ⚠ **Not medical advice and not a substitute for professional medical care.** Heat is a quiet
> killer; if you suspect heatstroke or a worsening heat illness, get help / call emergency services
> immediately.

---

## 1. What extreme heat is & why it matters

- Extreme heat is a period of **high heat + humidity above 90 °F for at least 2–3 days**; in extreme
  heat the body works extra hard to stay at normal temperature. **Extreme heat causes the highest
  number of annual deaths among all weather-related hazards.**
- Heat kills by pushing the body beyond its limits: in high heat + humidity, evaporation slows and
  the body must work harder. **Older adults, young children, people with certain illnesses/chronic
  conditions, and those who are overweight are at greater risk.** **Urban areas** can bake via the
  heat-island effect (asphalt/concrete store heat, released at night).

---

## 2. Key terms (FEMA)

| Term | Meaning |
|------|---------|
| **Heatwave** | Prolonged period of excessive heat, often with high humidity. |
| **Heat Index** | How hot it *feels* when humidity is added to air temp; full sunshine can add **15 °F**. |
| **Heat Cramps** | Muscle pains/spasms from heavy exertion — often the *first* sign the body is struggling with heat. |
| **Heat Exhaustion** | Result of heavy exercise/work in heat + humidity with excess fluid loss — a mild shock; if untreated it can worsen to heatstroke. |
| **Heatstroke** | **Life-threatening.** The body's cooling (sweating) system stops working; body temp can rise enough to cause brain damage/death if not cooled quickly. |

---

## 3. Who's at risk (CDC / NOAA)

Higher risk: **people over 65**, **infants and young children**, **pregnant women**, people with
**asthma or heart disease**, **chronic medical conditions**, and people working/exercising outdoors
in heat. Children/infants' bodies are *"less able to adapt to heat than adults."*

---

## 4. Before heat — preparation

- **Do not rely on a fan as your primary cooling** — *"fans create airflow and a false sense of
  comfort, but do not reduce body temperature."* Use them **only if indoor temps are below 90 °F**;
  above 90 °F a fan can raise body temperature. Use AC or find a cooled place (library, mall,
  cooling centre — dial 2-1-1).
- **Cool the home:** cover sun-facing windows with drapes/shades/reflectors (awnings/louvers can
  reduce heat entering by **up to 80%**); weather-strip doors/windows; add insulation; insulate
  window AC units; use an attic ventilator.
- **Plan for power loss:** extreme heat can cause outages — know the plan for refrigerated
  medications and electronic medical devices.

---

## 5. During heat — stay cool, hydrated, connected

- **Stay cool:** stay indoors/shade as much as possible; wear **loose, lightweight, light-coloured**
  clothing covering as much skin as possible + a wide-brimmed hat; take cool showers;
  **use the oven less**; avoid strenuous work in the hottest part of the day — **use a buddy system**
  and take frequent breaks.
- **Hydrate:** **drink plenty of water**; carry a water bottle; limit high-sugar/sodium/caffeine/
  alcohol drinks; check urine colour (light yellow/clear usually = enough). Some conditions need a
  doctor's advice before increasing fluids; don't take salt tablets unless directed.
- **Medicines:** *"many medicines can make you dehydrated or overheated"* — **don't stop or change
  them without your doctor**; store meds out of hot places.
- **Check on others:** family, older adults, neighbours without AC, people with chronic conditions
  or who live alone; and **pets** (cool water + shade).

---

## 6. Recognise & respond to heat illness (disclosure required)

**Heatstroke — a severe medical emergency (call 9-1-1 or get to a hospital immediately):**
signs — extremely high body temp (**above 103 °F**, Ready.gov; **105+** FEMA), **red/hot/dry skin
with no sweat**, rapid fast/weak pulse, dizziness/confusion/unconsciousness. Response — call 9-1-1
first; move to a cool shaded area; remove outer clothing; place a **cold, wet cloth/ice pack on
head, neck, armpits, groin** (or soak clothing); elevate the feet. *"Delay can be fatal."*

**Heat cramps:** muscle pains/spasms; cool down, remove excess clothing, sip water/sports drink.

**Heat exhaustion:** heavy sweating, paleness, muscle cramps, tiredness/weakness, fast or weak
pulse, dizziness, headache, fainting, nausea/vomiting. Cool down, sip water; **seek immediate
medical attention if vomiting occurs**; call 9-1-1 for a medical emergency. (Note: Ready.gov and
FEMA state slightly different temperature thresholds — both are attributed; a thermometer reading
isn't the whole picture, act on signs.)

## 7. Vehicles — never leave children or pets

- **It is NEVER safe** to leave a child, disabled person, or pet locked in a car — *"kids may play
  in cars or wander outside, get in, and **can die in 10 minutes**."* Lock your cars even in your
  own driveway with a toddler. (A reported 31 children died in hot cars in 2025 — re-verify
  currency before citing.)
