# Shelter & Home — Emergency Kit & First 72 Hours

> Category: **SHELTER** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary sources: **FEMA** — *Are You Ready?*; **Ready.gov — Build a Kit**. Verified 2026-09-08.
> **Universal reference — generic kit categories, never a filled household list.** Your own
> household's specific contents stay in the private household templates.

*"Basic services such as electricity, gas, water, sewage treatment, and telephones may be cut off
for **days, or even a week or longer**. Or you may have to evacuate at a moment's notice."* — after
a disaster you may need to **survive on your own for several days**; relief can't reach everyone
immediately.

---

## 1. Water — the flagship quantity

- **Store at least 1 gallon of water per person per day** (drinking + sanitation); a normally
  active person needs **at least ½ gallon daily just for drinking**.
- **Children, nursing mothers, and ill people need more**; **very hot conditions can double the
  need**. A 3-day baseline = **1 gal/person/day** for several days.
- **Storage:** commercially bottled water is safest (keep sealed, note the use-by date). If using
  your own containers: use **2-litre plastic soft-drink bottles** — **not** jugs/cardboard that held
  milk or fruit juice (residues breed bacteria), **not** glass. Sanitise with **1 tsp non-scented
  bleach per quart**; **replace stored water every 6 months**.

---

## 2. The emergency supply kit — universal categories

**Basic kit (Ready.gov / FEMA recommended minimum):**
- Water (1 gal/person/day)
- Non-perishable food (several days; avoid salty/drying foods — choose salt-free crackers, whole
  grain cereals, canned food with high liquid content)
- **Battery-powered or hand-crank radio + a NOAA Weather Radio with tone alert**
- **Flashlight** + extra batteries
- **First aid kit** + manual
- **Whistle** (signal for help)
- **Dust mask** (filter contaminated air) + **plastic sheeting, scissors, duct tape** (shelter in place)
- **Moist towelettes, garbage bags, plastic ties** (sanitation)
- **Wrench/pliers** (turn off utilities) + **manual can opener**
- **Local maps**; cell phone + chargers + backup battery
- **Cash or traveller's cheques**; photocopies of ID / credit cards

**Individual-need items (ready.gov):** prescription meds + extra, over-the-counter meds,
eyeglasses/contact solution, infant formula/bottles/diapers, pet food + water, sleeping bag/blanket,
change of clothing + sturdy shoes, fire extinguisher, matches in a waterproof container, feminine/
personal-hygiene supplies, mess kits/utensils, paper/pencil, games for children.

**Cold-climate extras (FEMA):** one complete change of clothing + shoes per person (jacket, long
pants, long-sleeve shirt, sturdy shoes, hat, mittens, scarf), sleeping bag/warm blanket per person.

---

## 3. Where to keep kits — home, work, car

- **Home:** a kit ready to *"grab and go"* if evacuated — in a designated place everyone knows.
- **Work:** be prepared to shelter **at least 24 hours** — food, water, medicines, delight shoes.
- **Car:** a kit in case you're stranded.
- **Packaging:** store items in airtight bags in **one or two easy-to-carry containers** (plastic
  bins / duffel bag).
- **Maintain:** keep canned food cool and dry; store boxed food in sealed containers; replace
  expired items; **re-think needs every year** as the family changes.

---

## 4. When to grab the kit / evacuate (decision aid)

**Evacuate when** — directed by local authorities, or you: live in a mobile home/temporary
structure; live in a high-rise (winds higher aloft); live on the coast/floodplain/near a river or
waterway; **or feel you are in danger.** Many disasters allow **no time** to gather necessities.

**If evacuation seems likely:** keep a **full tank of gas** (stations may be closed / no power);
plan **one car per family**; wear sturdy shoes + long pants/sleeves + cap; listen to a battery radio
and follow local instructions.

**If time permits:** grab the kit; secure the home (close/lock doors + windows, unplug electrical
equipment, **leave freezers/refrigerators plugged in unless flooding risk**); let others know where
you're going; **leave early enough to avoid being trapped**; **follow recommended routes — no
shortcuts**; **never drive into flooded areas.**
