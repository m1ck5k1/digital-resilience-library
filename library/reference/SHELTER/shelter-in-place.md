# Shelter & Home — Shelter-in-Place (When to Stay & How)

> Category: **SHELTER** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary source: **Ready.gov — Shelter** (US DHS/FEMA). Verified 2026-09-08 (Wayback replay).
> **Not a substitute for local authorities' instructions.** "Sheltering in place" means staying
> where you are to avoid an outside hazard — it can be minutes (a tornado warning) or longer.

---

## 1. What "shelter" means

Sheltering is appropriate when conditions require protection **at your home, work, or another
location** when emergencies arise. The time may be short (a tornado warning) or extended. It can
be one of three:
- **Stay-at-Home**
- **Going to a Mass Care Shelter**
- **Sheltering in Place** (below)

During extended sheltering, **manage water and food supplies** so you have what you need.

---

## 2. Shelter-in-place — when it's the right call

Consider it when it's **best to stay where you are and avoid uncertainty outside**. Indicators:
- You see **large amounts of debris in the air**, or
- Local authorities say the **air is badly contaminated**.

Notably, *"local authorities may not immediately be able to provide information"* — so be
prepared to act on common sense + available information.

---

## 3. Shelter-in-place — the practical steps

1. **Pay attention to local media** for official news/instructions as they arrive.
2. **Bring your family and pets inside.**
3. **Lock doors; close windows, air vents, and fireplace dampers.**
4. **Turn off fans, air conditioning, and forced-air heating systems** (so outside air isn't drawn in).
5. **Take your emergency supply kit** unless it may be contaminated.
6. **Go into an interior room with few windows** if possible.
7. **Seal the room** (see below).

### Sealing a room (for contaminated outside air)
This is a **temporary** protective barrier and requires pre-planning — **plastic sheeting + duct
tape** kept in your kit:
- Cut plastic sheeting **several inches wider** than each opening; measure and **pre-cut + label**
  sheets in advance to save time.
- Duct-tape the plastic at the **corners first**, then tape down **all edges**.
- Be ready to **improvise** and use what you have to seal gaps and create a barrier to contamination.

---

## 4. Stay-at-Home (and a note on "stay-at-home" sheltering)

- **Remain indoors as much as possible**; you can still use patios/porches/yards.
- Outdoor activities (walking, jogging) are fine; essential errands (grocery, pharmacy, post
  office) are fine.
- **Limit visitors** if possible; use video chat; call people you'd normally text.

---

## 5. Important limits

- **Shelter-in-place is not always right.** If officials order an **evacuation**, leave — don't
  shelter inside a building that's unsafe (e.g. mandatory evacuation zones). Sealing a room protects
  from contaminated *air*, not from fire, flood, or structural collapse.
- **Deciding when to stay vs leave** depends on the specific hazard and local instructions — this
  doc gives the stay-in-place method; your evacuation plan is a separate, equally practised one.
