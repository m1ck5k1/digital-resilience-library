# Legal & Records — Important Papers & Records Guide

> Category: **LEGAL** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary source concept: **FEMA Emergency Financial First Aid Kit (EFFAK)** and federal
> personal-preparedness guidance (Ready.gov). **Not legal advice.** This is a general
> organization and preparedness guide. Laws and document requirements differ by jurisdiction
> and situation — for a specific need, consult a qualified attorney or other professional.
> It deliberately contains **no identifying data**; the matching blank inventory is
> [`LEGAL-important-papers-inventory.md`](../templates/LEGAL-important-papers-inventory.md).

In a crisis, people are pulled in many directions — and the documents they need most are often
scattered or lost with their home. The cheapest resilience investment is a **records kit**:
knowing what you have, where it is, and being able to grab it.

---

## 1. Why this matters (and why it's not "legal advice")

This library's legal category exists so a household can **function** under stress: find proof of
identity and insurance, continue essential financial/health arrangements, and reconstruct their
lives after a fire, flood, or evacuation. It is **not** a substitute for a lawyer on a
specific legal problem. Use this guide to *organize*; use a professional for anything that
turns into a legal dispute, claim, or legal action.

A good rule: **if a document would be hard or slow to replace, keep a copy you can reach
quickly.**

---

## 2. The records kit — three buckets

Think of your important papers in three groups, each with different priorities:

### A. Identity & personal
- Government-issued photo ID (passport, driver licence / state ID), birth certificate,
  social-security / national-ID card (keep the card **separate from your wallet**).
- Marriage / divorce, adoption, citizenship or naturalization records.
- Passwords / access to secure digital accounts.

### B. Financial
- Bank, credit-union and brokerage account numbers + branch contact.
- Recent statements (last 1–2 months of each account).
- Insurance policies (home/renters, auto, life, health) — **policy numbers + the claims line**.
- Mortgages, leases, loans, and vehicle titles.
- Tax summaries, payroll/income records.
- For the self-employed / small household: business registrations, licences, client/payroll
  records, and supplier contacts.

> This is the **Emergency Financial First Aid Kit (EFFAK)** idea: a pre-assembled, portable
> snapshot (a "financial inventory") you can hand to a claims agent or an advisor and use to
> keep bills-and-money life running when mail/online access is interrupted.

### C. Health & directives
- Health insurance cards + provider contacts.
- Current medication list and any standing prescriptions/allergies.
- Living will / advance directive, health-care proxy/power of attorney, organ-donation record,
  wills and trusts — **store the original where your executor/family can find it, and tell
  them where that is.**

---

## 3. Where to keep it

- **Keep one copy at home** in a fire-/water-resistant container (a "grab-and-go" folder or safe).
- **Keep a second copy off-site** — a trusted person's home, a bank box, or secure digital
  backup (encrypted). 3-2-1: **three** copies, **two** formats, **one** off-site.
- **Back up digitally** (scanned/photo copies) in an encrypted store; note that a photo of an
  ID is *not* a legal original — it's for reference and reconstruction speed.
- **Share locations deliberately.** In a medical emergency an adult who knows where the
  advance directive is matters more than the paper itself.

---

## 4. When you must evacuate

- Take the **documents kit** (if ready) — identity + insurance + essential-contact list first.
- If you have minutes: grab **phones, chargers, ID, wallet, meds, keys** before anything else.
- Note what you're leaving behind so you can report losses accurately.

---

## 5. Checks & refresh

- Review the kit **twice a year** (e.g. at clock-change time) and update anything stale.
- Update immediately after any big change: address move, marriage/divorce, new insurance, new
  loan, job change.
- Confirm a **trusted person outside your immediate household** knows what's in your kit and
  where it lives.
