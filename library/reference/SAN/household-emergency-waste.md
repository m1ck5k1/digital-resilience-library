# Sanitation — Household & Emergency Waste

> Category: **SAN** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary sources: **WHO** — *Manual on Water and Environmental Sanitation for Disaster
> Management* (2006); **Ready.gov — Returning Home**. Verified 2026-09-08.
> **Not a substitute for local waste/rubbish rules or professional guidance.** This is the
> general solid-waste / soiled-laundry / hygienic-waste layer (the excreta and hand-hygiene
> basics live in separate SAN docs).

---

## 1. Goal & household standard (WHO)

The goal is an environment *"acceptably uncontaminated by solid waste, including medical waste,"*
with a convenient, effective disposal means. The household standard:
- Household waste is put in containers **daily** for collection, or **burnt/buried in a specified
  refuse pit**.
- Every household has a refuse container **and/or is no more than 100 m from a communal refuse
  pit**.
- At least **one 100-litre refuse container with a fly-proof cover per 10 families**, where refuse
  isn't buried on-site.

**Why it matters:** if organic waste isn't disposed of, the risks are **fly/rat breeding** and
**surface-water pollution**; uncollected waste and debris *"often block drainage channels,"* causing
stagnant water.

---

## 2. Collection

- **Household level:** as a rule of thumb, **one 100–200-litre container** (plastic or metal, with
  a **tight-fitting lid**) per **10–20 families**, placed **not more than 15 m from the shelter**.
- **Market/commercial areas:** larger containers or collection bays; some liquid wastes (fish/
  animal slaughter) need special containers.
- **Workforce:** about **2 workers per 1,000 residents** may be needed for street/site cleaning,
  collection, and transfer.

---

## 3. On-site burial — pits & trenches (WHO)

- If buried on-site, **cover at least weekly with a 20–30 cm soil layer** to deter flies.
- **Fence disposal sites** to prevent accidents and access by children, rag-pickers, animals.
- **In low-density settlements**, dig **per-family** refuse pits.
- **Communal refuse trench:** **1.5 m wide, 2 m deep**; cover each day with ~**20 cm** of earth;
  when the level is **40 cm below ground, fill + compact and dig a new trench**.
- **A 1-metre trench per ~200 residents fills in about a week.**
- **Separate biodegradable (vegetable) refuse** into one trench, **non-biodegradable** (bottles,
  cans, plastic) into another. The biodegradable trench can be dug out after **6 months** as **compost**.
- **Cans/bottles** may be cleaned and recycled; **never reuse containers that held dangerous
  chemicals (e.g. pesticides)** — crush so they can't be reused and **bury far from any water
  source**.

---

## 4. Landfill & incineration (WHO)

- **Sanitary landfill** is usually the best final disposal: located **away from the settlement**,
  on vacant/uncultivated land, in natural depressions, **downwind**, and arranged to avoid
  surface/groundwater pollution.
- **Incineration** is a third option, **but is not usually suitable for menstrual cloths/sanitary
  napkins**. Place incinerators away from the settlement (opposite the prevailing wind) on an
  **impervious base**; **bury ash and unburned refuse under 40 cm of soil**.

---

## 5. Waste segregation at source (WHO)

Separate:
- **Bio-contaminated wastes** (including sharp materials),
- **Chemical wastes** (drugs, chemical solutions — including **chemical-product containers**),
- **Common wastes** (paper, cardboard, glass).

In living/public areas there must be **no contaminated or dangerous medical wastes** (needles,
glass, dressings, drugs) at any time.

---

## 6. Menstrual / hygienic waste (WHO)

*"Women and girls who menstruate should have access to suitable materials for the absorption and
disposal of menstrual blood"* — and should be consulted on what is **culturally appropriate**.

---

## 7. Liquid waste / greywater / laundry (WHO)

- **Do not let standing pools of polluted wastewater form** (from washing, food prep, wasted tap
  water) — they **encourage mosquito breeding**; keep children out of hazardous pools.
- Provide **soakage pits** for liquid waste.
- Wastewater from **bathing/clothes-washing should NOT go into the latrine pit** — dispose of it
  separately (soakage).
- Where relevant, distinguish water for drinking from water for bathing, laundry, and animals.

---

## 8. Post-flood home cleanup (Ready.gov)

- **Do not return home before the area is declared safe** by local officials.
- **Water & sewage:** if pipes are damaged, turn off the main water valve; **check with local
  authorities before using any water**; pump out wells and have the water **tested** before
  drinking; **do not flush toilets until you know sewage lines are intact**.
- **Food:** **throw out all food and supplies** you suspect may be contaminated or touched floodwater.
- **Flooded basement:** pump it out **gradually** (~one-third per day) to avoid wall collapse /
  floor buckling while the ground is still waterlogged.
- **Cleaning:** clean up chemical spills; **disinfect items contaminated by raw sewage, bacteria,
  or chemicals**; salvage what can be cleaned.
