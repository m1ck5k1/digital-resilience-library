# Sanitation & Hygiene — Hand Washing (with Limited Water & Soap vs Ash)

> Category: **SAN** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary source: **WHO** emergency-health guidance. Verified live 2026-09-07.
> **Not a substitute for professional / current guidance.**

Hand-washing is the single cheapest, most effective hygiene action in survival and
emergency settings. It blocks the faecal–oral route that drives most of the diarrhoeal
disease burden.

---

## 1. When to wash (the critical moments)

Everyone in the household should wash hands:
- **After defecating**;
- **After cleaning a child who has defecated** (and disposing of the stool);
- **Before preparing food**;
- **Before eating**;
- **Before feeding a child**.

Adults and older children should also **wash young children's hands** at these key moments
(children's hands spread infection too).

---

## 2. What to wash with — soap, or ash, and water

- **Soap and water** is first choice.
- **Ash is an accepted alternative** to soap when soap isn't available — a recognised
  substitute in emergency and resource-limited settings. (Ash + water provides the friction
  and mild alkalinity that help lift and remove germs.)
- There should be a **source of water near the toilet** so people can wash immediately after
  defecating.

Key point: the priority is washing **at the critical moments** — even with limited water, a
small amount used at the right times (especially after defecation and before eating) breaks
the chain of disease far more effectively than generous water used at random times.

---

## 3. Why it matters (context)

- **Hand-washing with soap**, together with safe drinking-water and improved sanitation, is a
  core prevention measure for **diarrhoeal disease**.
- Inadequate water, sanitation, and hygiene (WASH) underlie a large share of preventable
  death globally each year; unsafe sanitation alone accounts for a substantial fraction.
