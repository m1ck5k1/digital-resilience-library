# Sanitation & Hygiene — Emergency Water Quantity & Safe Water

> Category: **SAN** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary sources: **Ready.gov / FEMA** (quantity), **WHO** emergency-health guidance,
> **EPA** (safe water). Verified live 2026-09-07.
> **Not a substitute for professional / current emergency-guidance advice.**

This doc is about the **hygiene side of water**: how much water a person actually needs,
and how to make water safe enough for drinking and hand-washing when regular supply is cut.
It complements (not duplicates) the WATER category — see `library/reference/WATER/`.

---

## 1. How much water to store / provide per person

The US federal baseline (Ready.gov):
- **Store at least 1 gallon (≈3.8 L) of water per person per day**, for drinking and
  sanitation combined — plan for several days, ideally a 2-week supply.
- A normally active person needs about **3/4 gallon (≈2.8 L) of fluid daily** (from water and
  beverages).
- **Children, nursing mothers, and sick people may need more**; in very hot conditions water
  needs can **double**.
- **Never ration drinking water** unless authorities direct it.

The fuller WHO planning breakdown gives the same picture in more detail. Recommended
**total ≈ 15–20 L per person per day**, split roughly:
- Drinking **3–4 L**
- Food preparation & cleanup **2–3 L**
- Personal hygiene **6–7 L**
- Laundry **4–6 L**

Sphere humanitarian-indicator rules of thumb (for a camp/shelter setting):
- **≥15 L per person per day** collected;
- **≥1 water point per 250 people**; max **500 m** shelter→point; max **15 min** queuing;
- flow **≥0.125 L/s** per point.

Household planning formula some agencies use: **15 L × number of people × number of days**.

## 2. Making water safe (drinking + hygiene)

Same methods as the WATER category — the essentials:

- **Boil**: filter cloudy water first (settle, then clean cloth/paper towel/coffee filter),
  then **bring to a rolling boil ≥ 1 minute** (EPA); **at altitude above ~5,000 ft / 1,500 m,
  boil 3 minutes**. Boiling will **not** remove heavy metals, salts, or most other chemicals.
- **Household bleach** (unscented chlorine, ~6% or ~8.25% sodium hypochlorite): a common
  emergency dose is roughly **6–8 drops per gallon** — but **always read the label/current
  guidance and let it stand ~30 minutes** before drinking. Double the dose for cloudy or very
  cold water. Use only fresh (<1 yr) unscented disinfecting bleach.
- **Tablets/drops**: follow the package label exactly for dose and contact time.

> **Two-part rule for hygiene:** water for hand-washing and drinking should both be made safe
> the same way — boiling or chlorine — when the supply is uncertain. Bacteria, viruses, and
> parasites travel through unwashed hands as easily as through dirty water.

---

## 3. Why hygiene water matters (context)

- **≥1.7 billion people** (2022) use a drinking-water source contaminated with faeces; the
  diseases this transmits cause an estimated **~505,000 diarrhoeal deaths/year**.
- Of inadequate WASH (water, sanitation, hygiene) deaths, a large share is from
  **unsafe sanitation** specifically.
- **Safe and sufficient water facilitates hygiene**, which is a key measure against not just
  diarrhoeal disease but also acute respiratory infections and many neglected tropical
  diseases.
