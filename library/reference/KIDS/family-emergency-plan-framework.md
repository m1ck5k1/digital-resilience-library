# Family & Kids — Family Emergency Plan Framework

> Category: **KIDS** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary source concept: **Ready.gov / FEMA "Make a Plan"** and child-focused preparedness
> guidance. **General guidance.** Every serious step below is generic — this page contains
> **no identifying data**. The matching blank scaffold to fill in with YOUR family's details
> is [`KIDS-family-emergency-plan.md`](../templates/KIDS-family-emergency-plan.md). Fill it
> locally; your answers never enter this repo.

A family plan turns a confusing emergency into a set of known steps. Kids cope better when
they have a **role** and a **routine** — the plan is how you give them both.

---

## 1. Know the risks that actually hit your area

- For your region, name the most likely events (e.g. severe storms, floods, wildfires, heat,
  winter storms, power outages). You can't plan for everything; plan for the *likely* + the
  *life-safety basics* (fire, evacuation, the "everyone find each other" case).

---

## 2. Agree on meeting places (three, in order)

- **In the home** — for sudden events (fire/storm) where you shelter in place.
- **Just outside the home** — for a fast evacuation from the house (the mailbox/fence/street
  sign, so it's safe to approach near many hazards).
- **Outside the neighbourhood** — for an area-wide event where the home may be inaccessible.
- Write where these are into the **filled** plan (blank template, kept with you) — not here.

---

## 3. Choose emergency contacts

- **One contact outside your immediate area** (long-distance calls often route when local
  lines are congested) and **one local**.
- **Teach kids the numbers they can reach**, and that a **text or a call from a friend's
  phone** is acceptable. Teach them your full names and home address/area.

---

## 4. Give each child a role (age-appropriate)

- Small jobs build calm competence: "open the door," "hold the go-bag," "bring the pets,"
  "check the smoke in the hallway."
- **One person is always in charge of each child** in mixed-adult groups (assign, don't
  assume — in a crowd everyone assumes someone else is watching).
- For older kids: first-aid help, helping younger siblings, calling a pre-agreed contact.

---

## 5. One bag, one practice, repeated

- Kids' **go-bags**: comfort item, favourite book/toy, water, snack, a small change of
  clothes, something to write on. A beloved item is a real de-escalator.
- **Practice the plan** — a fire drill at home, a "meet-time" walk to the outside/neighbourhood
  meeting spots, and running through "who calls whom." Practice is what makes it work under
  stress.
- **Schools and childcare:** learn the facility's own evacuation/relocation procedures and
  where official updates get posted; make sure the emergency-contact info on file is current.

---

## 6. Talking about it (the emotional layer)

- **Keep it honest and short.** Use plain words matched to the child's age; "we'll stay calm,
  we'll follow our plan, an adult will tell you what to do."
- **Name the plan's job:** the plan exists so you can feel scared *and* still know what to do.
  It's okay to be scared; it's not okay to have no next step.
- **Normal rules for parents:** model calm; don't assign your own fear to them; after an
  event, give them a task and a debrief ("what worked?"), and watch for clinging, regressions,
  or sleep changes that signal extra support is needed.
- **Comfort items are allowed.** Letting a child keep their comfort item in an emergency is
  not weakness; it conserves their coping capacity.

---

## 7. Keep the plan alive

- Revisit **twice a year** and after any move, new baby, school change, or change in who
  lives in the household.
- **Confirm kids remember** the two contacts and the three meeting places every so often —
  memory, not just the paper, is the goal.
