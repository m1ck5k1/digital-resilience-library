# Security & Safety — Digital & Online Safety Awareness

---

## 1. The four easy ways (CISA Secure Our World)

CISA frames everyday online safety as **four easy ways**:
1. **Recognize and Report Phishing** — *"often tries to get us to open a harmful attachment or
   share personal information."*
2. **Stop Online Crime with Strong Passwords** — *"using strong passwords and a password manager
   are some easy ways to protect ourselves."*
3. **Make your accounts safer with Multifactor Authentication (MFA)** — using **more than a
   password** to access an account (a text code, an authenticator app, or a fingerprint).
4. **Update Software for Safety** — *"don't delay software updates. Flaws in software can give
   criminals access… we must install updates for the latest protection."*

---

## 2. Recognize & report phishing (CISA)

Phishing = criminals trying to get you to open harmful links/emails/attachments that request
personal info or infect your device, made to look like a trusted person/organisation.

- **Recognize:** common signs — urgent or emotionally-appealing language claiming dire
  consequences; requests to send personal/financial info; untrusted shortened URLs; incorrect
  email addresses or links (even one wrong character); poor grammar. **But with AI, some emails
  have perfect grammar** — watch for the other signs too.
- **Resist:** if you suspect phishing, don't click links/attachments that seem too good to be
  true; report it (using the report option near the sender or a "report spam" button).
- **Delete:** delete the message; don't reply or click any attachment, link, **or even the
  "unsubscribe" link**.
- **To verify a suspicious message:** don't click any link or call any number in it — go to the
  company's real website for contact info, or reach the person another way (e.g. phone the friend
  who supposedly messaged you).

---

## 3. Strong passwords + password managers (CISA; NIST)

A strong password follows **all three**:
1. **Make them long** — **at least 16 characters** (CISA); NIST requires **at least 8** (and
   recommends allowing up to 64). Longer is stronger.
2. **Make them random** — a random string of mixed-case letters, numbers, symbols; **or** a
   memorable **passphrase of 5–7 unrelated words**, strengthened with creative spelling and/or a
   number/symbol.
3. **Make them unique** — a different strong password for each account.

- **Use a password manager** — it stores all your passwords, flags weak/reused ones, can generate
  strong ones, and auto-fills logins. *"We only need to remember one strong password — the one for
  the password manager itself."* Free built-in browser managers exist; read reviews to choose a
  reputable one.
- **Do NOT save passwords "in a file on a computer"** (CISA).
- NIST notes password managers are widely used and *"in many cases increase the likelihood that
  users will choose stronger"* secrets; NIST does **not** require forced special-character rules
  beyond length.

---

## 4. Turn on MFA (CISA)

MFA confirms identity at login (a code texted to a phone, one generated by an authenticator app,
or a fingerprint).
- **Why:** *"Even if our passwords become compromised, unauthorized users will be unable to meet
  the second step requirement and will not be able to access our accounts."*
- **Per account:** (1) go to Settings; (2) find and turn on MFA (may be called two-factor /
  two-step authentication); (3) choose a method — text/email code, **authenticator app (a new code
  every ~30 seconds)**, or biometrics.
- **Turn on MFA for every account/app that offers it** — email, banking, social media, work.

---

## 5. Offline, encrypted, tested backups (CISA & MS-ISAC Ransomware Guide)

- *"It is critical to maintain **offline, encrypted backups** of data and to **regularly test your
  backups**."*
- Backups should be ongoing and **kept offline** — *"many ransomware variants attempt to find and
  delete any accessible backups."*
- **Why it matters:** *"there is no need to pay a ransom for data that is readily accessible."*
- (The library's own **3-2-1 backup ethos** is a companion framing; CISA prescribes
  offline/encrypted/tested rather than using the literal "3-2-1" label.)
