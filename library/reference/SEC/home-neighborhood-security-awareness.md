## 1. Why awareness matters — and what "security" includes

Security isn't only locks and cameras — it begins with **life-safety** (fire, carbon monoxide)
and with how spaces are **designed and used**. Two frames organise it:

- **Life-safety first:** smoke and carbon-monoxide alarms are the single most important
  "security" devices in a home. A fire is not a distant threat: *"a fire can become
  life-threatening in just two minutes; a residence can be engulfed in flames in five
  minutes,"* and *"smoke and toxic gases kill more people than flames do"* (Ready.gov).
- **CPTED (crime prevention through environmental design):** a system for designing the built
  environment to *"reduce the possibility of opportunistic crime and limit the perception of
  crime in a neighborhood"* — a way of thinking rooted in how space is created, used, lit, and
  maintained (NCPC; Wikipedia).

## 2. Smoke alarms & escape — life-safety basics (Ready.gov)

- **Smoke-is-danger basics:** in *"less than 30 seconds a small flame can turn into a major
  fire"*; room temperatures in a fire can be **100 °F at floor level** and rise to
  **600 °F at eye level**.
- **Alarm upkeep:** replace batteries **twice a year** (unless 10-year lithium batteries);
  install smoke alarms **on every level including the basement**; **replace the entire unit
  every 10 years** or per manufacturer; **never disable a smoke alarm while cooking**.
- **Accessibility:** audible alarms exist for people with visual impairment; alarms with a
  **vibrating pad or flashing light** are available for people with hearing impairment.
- **Escape planning:** find **two ways out of each room**; make sure windows aren't stuck,
  screens come out quickly, and **security bars can be properly opened**; **practice your home
  fire escape plan twice a year**.

> Note: operable security bars are exactly the **life-safety ↔ hardening** intersection — good
> to keep in mind when adding any window bars (they must never block an emergency exit).

## 3. Carbon monoxide (CO) awareness (EPA)

- **CO is odorless, colorless, and toxic** — *"impossible to see, taste, or smell"*; it can kill
  before you're aware it's present.
- **Typical levels:** homes **without** gas stoves average **0.5–5 ppm**; near **properly
  adjusted** gas stoves often **5–15 ppm**; near **poorly adjusted** stoves may be **30 ppm or
  higher**.
- **Common sources:** unvented kerosene/gas space heaters; leaking chimneys and furnaces;
  back-drafting from furnaces/water heaters/wood stoves/fireplaces; gas stoves; **generators
  and other gasoline-powered equipment**; auto exhaust from attached garages; tobacco smoke.
- **Reduction:** maintain/adjust combustion equipment; use an exhaust fan vented outdoors over
  gas stoves; open flues when a fireplace is in use; have a trained professional inspect/clean/
  tune the central heating system.

## 4. CPTED — design that discourages crime (NCPC; Wikipedia)

- **CPTED** looks at *"the creation of space, its use and safety; the locations of land uses;
  the positions of buildings; interior and exterior design (color, lighting, entrances/exits,
  landscaping); and the users of space and when/how they use it."*
- **It is not just hardening.** Traditional measures (bars, alarms, cameras, gates) *"still
  have a legitimate, even vital, role. Beyond certain levels, however, hardening … can be
  expensive and disruptive. While alarms, cameras, and guards can mask the symptoms, they may
  never resolve the problem if the building's location or design provide criminals an
  opportunity."*
- **Six strategies** (per Moffat, via Wikipedia): **territoriality, natural surveillance,
  access control, image/maintenance, activity support, and target hardening.**
  - **Natural surveillance** — *"maximising the visibility of the space and its users"* so
    potential offenders are more likely to be seen.
  - **Natural access control** — *"distinguishing public from private space"* through the
    selective placement of entrances, fencing, lighting, and landscape to control flow.
  - **Territorial reinforcement** — promoting a sense of ownership / social control.
  - **Image & maintenance** — *"deterioration indicates less control by a site's intended users
    and a greater tolerance of disorder"* (the "broken window" idea).
- **The core deterrence principle:** *"the decision to offend … is more influenced by cues to
  the perceived risk of being caught than by cues to reward or ease of entry. The certainty of
  being caught is the central deterrence."*

## 5. Entry-point awareness (property-security knowledge)

General, non-identifying security knowledge about door/window design (Wikipedia):

- **Deadbolts:** unlike a spring latch, a deadbolt's mechanism *"prevents the bolt from being
  retracted by force, or shimming, and it cannot be moved to the open position except by
  rotating the key."*
- **Frames & strike plates:** reinforcing the **strike plate and/or door frame** *"prevents the
  strike plate from being rammed out of the frame."* **Longer/specialised hinge screws** can
  prevent a door being pulled out after hinge pins are removed.
- **Secondary internal locks** (sliding bolts, hooks, metal bars) mount internally.
- **Door windows:** security bars/grates, security films, or breakage-resistant glazing.
- **Visibility:** most police departments recommend **clearing shrubs from near doorways** so a
  burglar can't be hidden from public view.
- **Residential door types:** solid-wood, panel (hollow/solid core), metal-skinned or
  metal-edge-wrapped, and fibre-glass doors. In one test cited, **steel-edged doors**
  outperformed wood-edged ones *"by a ratio of 7 to 1"* in a 100 lb-force test, and *"practically
  two-thirds of all illegal entries were made through doors."*
- **Window barriers** include security glass, window locks, grilles, and shutters; EU standards
  (EN 1627–1630) classify closing-system resistance into **six levels**.

> **Evidence note (Chula Vista Residential Burglary Reduction Project, 1,000+ incidents):**
> methods found to have **relatively low** effectiveness included **sliding-glass-door wooden
> dowels (vs. pin/channel locks), deadbolts on the front door only (vs. all entry doors), and
> dusk-to-dawn-timer outdoor lights (vs. targeted/motion-activated lighting).** The takeaway is
> universal: **prioritise real locks (pin/channel), harden all entry doors, and use
> motion-targeted lighting** — not just the front door or timer lights.

## 6. Home inventory — document your possessions (III)

An up-to-date inventory *"help[s] settle insurance claims faster, verify losses for tax
returns, and purchase the correct amount of insurance."*

- **List:** describe each item, where bought, make/model; keep receipts for major items; record
  **serial numbers** for appliances/electronics.
- **Photos:** photograph rooms and important items; note what's shown and where bought.
- **Walk-through video:** *"walk through your house videotaping and describing the contents…
  open a kitchen shelf or closet and describe the contents."*
- **Store it OFF-SITE:** physical copy in a safe-deposit box / with a trusted person; digital
  copy backed up on an external drive or online storage.
- **Keep it current:** add significant purchases while details are fresh.

> ⚠ Your actual inventory contents are **household data** — keep them with you (per the DRL
> rule), not in this public library. The vendor's own "Know Your Stuff" tool is a commercial
> tie-in; the general method above stands independent of it.
