# FOOD — Preservation without Power

> Category: **FOOD** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> General method guidance for extending food when you can't rely on the grid or a freezer.
> Where a method has **exact safety-critical parameters** (canning times/pressures, acidity),
> they are **not quoted here** — they are routed to `sources-and-safe-hold.md` because getting
> them wrong is dangerous. Confirm before using these techniques at scale.

## The goal
Extend food's usable life using methods that need **no electricity** so a household can carry
a surplus through an outage rather than watch it spoil.

## Methods (general principle for each)

### Drying / dehydrating
- Removes moisture so microbes can't grow. Works for fruit, vegetables, herbs, meat (jerky),
  and grains.
- Sunny/air drying is simple; a solar or low-heat dehydrator speeds it up.
- **Keep dried food airtight and dark** — moisture and light undo it.
- Method safety note: meat drying has a **specific safe process** — confirm before attempting.

### Canning (heat sealing)
- High-heat processing kills microbes and seals jars airtight.
- **Two distinct processes:**
  - **Water-bath canning** — only safe for **high-acid** foods (many fruits, pickled items,
    tomatoes *with added acid*).
  - **Pressure canning** — required for **low-acid** foods (most vegetables, meats, soups).
- **CANNOT be improvised safely:** the time + pressure per food is exact and non-negotiable.
  Using the wrong process for a low-acid food risks botulism. **Do not attempt pressure-canning
  low-acid food without following verified current USDA/extension tables** (`sources-and-safe-hold.md`).

### Fermenting
- Lactic-acid fermentation preserves vegetables (sauerkraut, kimchi, pickles) via brine and
  beneficial bacteria, at room temperature.
- Needs salt brine + a clean, anaerobic environment. Does not need electricity.
- Result is a living, refrigerated-after-open product; follow a verified recipe.

### Root-cellar / cool storage
- Some vegetables (potatoes, onions, hard squash, carrots, apples) keep for months in a
  **cool, humid, dark** cellar or similar space — no processing needed.
- The cellar must be **cool (near but above freezing), humid, dark, well-ventilated**.

### Salting / sugaring / smoking
- Salt and sugar draw out moisture and inhibit microbes; smoking adds preservation + flavour.
- Works well combined with drying. Confirmation needed for **curing/smoking meat** specifics.

### Seed-saving (for future growing)
- Save seed from open-pollinated/non-hybrid plants; dry thoroughly; store **cool, dry, dark.
- Label variety + year. See the `FOOD-pantry-plan.md` template (seed store section).

## The key safety message
- **Some preservation is safe to improvise (drying, fermenting, root cellar).**
- **Canning — especially pressure-canning low-acid foods — and meat curing are NOT.** Their
  safety depends on exact, verified processes. When in doubt: don't guess, confirm against
  current USDA/extension guidance, or don't do it. Getting it wrong can cause serious food
  poisoning (botulism).
