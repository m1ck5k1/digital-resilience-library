# FOOD — Storage & Building a Pantry

> Category: **FOOD** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Written from well-attested, public-domain general guidance. Where an exact per-food
> shelf-life is load-bearing, it is flagged to confirm (see `sources-and-safe-hold.md`).
> Not a substitute for local/current food-safety guidance.

## 1. How much to hold

The standard emergency-preparedness baseline is a **minimum 3-day supply**, and a **2-week
supply** is the commonly recommended target for a household.

- Per person, a workable guide is a handful of **non-perishable, high-energy foods** that need
  little-to-no water to prepare.
- **Scale for your household** (adults, children, anyone with dietary/medical needs, pets).
- When you test/rotate, don't add to the pantry beyond what you'll actually eat — a pantry
  you won't eat is waste, not resilience.

## 2. What stores well (non-perishable / shelf-stable)

Prefer items that:
- Are **non-perishable** (don't need refrigeration before opening).
- **Keep well at cool, dry room temperature.**
- Are **high-energy / nutrient-dense** and hydrating (some produce water too — see WATER).

Common shelf-stable staples:
- Dry and canned grains (rice, pasta, oats), dry beans/lentils, canned vegetables and soups,
  canned/dried fruit, nut butters, cooking oil, salt, sugar/honey, dried milk, coffee/tea,
  and shelf-stable snacks.
- **Canned goods are the backbone** of a no-power pantry — they're pre-cooked and stay good
  longest at room temperature.

## 3. Storage conditions (well-attested rules of thumb)

| Condition | Why it matters |
|-----------|----------------|
| **Cool** (ideally below ~70°F / 21°C) | Heat accelerates spoilage and shortens shelf life; the cooler, the better. |
| **Dry** | Moisture breeds mold and pests. |
| **Dark** | Light degrades some vitamins and fats over time. |
| **Sealed / pest-tight** | Keep out insects and rodents; use airtight containers once packages are opened. |

> **Heat + light + moisture are the three enemies of stored food.** A cool, dry, dark,
> well-sealed store is the whole game for shelf-stable goods.

## 4. Rotation

- **First-in, first-out:** use the oldest stock first, replace with new.
- **Label** each stored item with the purchase/prep date.
- **Check periodically** (`FOOD-pantry-plan.md` has a review checklist): toss anything that is
  leaking, bulging, badly dented (seal compromised), or smells/tastes off.
- Rotating through your pantry in normal cooking keeps it fresh *and* means you always have
  stock you'd actually eat.

## 5. The "danger zone" rule (cold-food safety)

A core food-safety fact that applies to any food kept cold:
- Perishable food left in the **danger zone — between 40°F and 140°F (≈4°C and 60°C)** — for
  **more than 2 hours** should be discarded (and **1 hour** if the ambient temperature is above
  90°F / 32°C, e.g. outdoors in heat).
- This matters during a power outage for anything you took out of the fridge; an unpowered
  fridge keeps food safe only while it stays cold (see `ENERGY` category references for
  outage handling).
