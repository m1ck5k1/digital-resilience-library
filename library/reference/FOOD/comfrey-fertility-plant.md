# Recovered Knowledge - Comfrey as a Fertility Plant

> Recovered knowledge - once common, fell out of modern commodity practice, and genuinely valuable for long-term self-reliance. Every fact is source-grounded; no invention.

## 1. What Comfrey Is

Comfrey (Symphytum x uplandicum 'Bocking 14') - the sterile hybrid - is a dynamic accumulator: a deep-taproot mineral pump that pulls potassium (K), calcium (Ca), and other micronutrients from deep subsoil up into leaves. This active transport against a concentration gradient achieves higher nutrient concentration in the plant than in the surrounding soil, making it a self-repairing fertility element.

The Bocking 14 cultivar is commonly propagated by crown division (matches the sterile-hybrid reality) and is perennial - no annual replant needed.

## 2. Fertility Use

### Comfrey as a Gas-Independent N+K Source

- Comfrey tea (liquid feed): Chopped leaves soaked in water. An aerated 24-48h brew (using an air stone) extracts potassium and nitrogen efficiently; a classic slow soak (weeks) also works but risks anaerobic/stinky conditions.
- Comfrey is especially rich in potassium (K) and nitrogen (N) - potassium from comfrey is great for growing tomatoes.
- Gas-independent: Modern synthetic N+K fertilizer depends on the Haber-Bosch process, which uses fossil natural gas as feedstock. Comfrey provides a local, N+K liquid feed without fossil-gas inputs.

### Correct Caveat (sourced, not invented)

Comfrey contains pyrrolizidine alkaloids - compounds that are toxic if ingested. Comfrey should NOT be used for internal consumption or medicinal use. Its value is strictly as a fertiliser/mulch for soil building. Readers use it to feed the soil, not to drink.

## 3. How to Use It

- Chop-and-drop green mulch: Cut leaves and lay them on the soil surface around food crops; they break down and return nutrients.
- Comfrey tea liquid feed: Soak chopped leaves in water (aerated 24-48h or classic slow soak), then use the liquid to water food crops.
- Establishment: Propagate by crown division (matches the sterile Bocking-14 hybrid). Once established, it is perennial and spreads via root pieces - manage accordingly.
- Why it keeps building fertility: The deep taproot continuously pulls minerals from subsoil into leaves; chopping and dropping returns those minerals to the surface, cycling fertility without external inputs.
