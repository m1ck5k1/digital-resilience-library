# Navigation — Emergency Signaling (Be Seen, Be Heard)

> Category: **NAV** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary source: **US Army FM 21-76** (Survival), Ch.19 — public-domain US Government work.
> Verified 2026-09-07.
> **Not a substitute for a satellite communicator / personal locator beacon where one is
> available.** This is for when you must signal with what you have.

A rescuer can only find you if you make yourself **visible or audible**. The international
distress signal is **three** of anything — three fires, three smoke columns, three whistles,
three gunshots, three flashes.

---

## 1. Fire & smoke (visual, most effective by night)

- **At night, fire is the most effective visual signal.** Build **three fires in a triangle**
  (the international distress signal), or in a straight line with about **25 m between fires**.
  If working alone, maintain **ONE** signal fire.
- **Smoke:** the international distress signal is **three columns of smoke**.
  - **White smoke** — smother the fire with green leaves, moss, or a little water.
  - **Black smoke** — add rubber or oil-soaked rags.
  - Always **contrast the smoke against the background** (sky vs. treeline).

---

## 2. Signal mirror (best in daylight)

- The **signal mirror is the best signaling device on a sunny day**.
- **Do NOT flash rapidly** — a pilot may mistake it for enemy fire. **Don't direct the beam
  into the cockpit for more than a few seconds.**
- Range: *"Pilots have reported seeing mirror flashes up to 160 kilometers away under ideal
  conditions."*
- Get to the **highest point**; if you hear an aircraft but can't see it, **flash toward the
  sound**. Wear the mirror with the glass side against your body to keep it clean.

---

## 3. Flares & strobes (night)

- **Strobe light:** flashes **60 times per minute**; can send **SOS at night**.
- **Pen flare:** fires a flare about **150 m high**.
- **Star cluster:** **RED is the international distress color**; reaches **200–215 m**, burns
  **6–10 s** on average, descends at **14 m/s**.
- **Star parachute flares:** **200–215 m**; **M126 (red)** burns ~**50 s**, **M127 (white)**
  ~**25 s**; visible at night **48–56 km**.

---

## 4. Sound

- **Whistle:** in documented cases heard up to **1.6 km away**; manufactured whistles carry
  further than a human voice.
- **Gunshots:** **three shots at distinct intervals** = distress signal (not in enemy
  territory).
- **SOS:** *three dots, three dashes, three dots* — internationally recognised. Using signal
  flags: hold the **flag left for dashes, right for dots**.

---

## 5. Ground-to-air emergency code

- Make symbols at least **1 m wide and 6 m long** (a 1:6 ratio); if larger, keep the **same
  1:6 ratio** so they're recognisable from the air.
- **Five** distinct symbols form the emergency code — confirm the exact shapes before relying
  on them (the reference figures were not OCR-verified this pass; use a printed ground-to-air
  code card).

---

## 6. Improvised, highly-visible markers

- **VS-17 panel:** orange side up is easier to see from the air than violet; any **bright
  orange or violet cloth** substitutes.
- **Sea dye markers:** conspicuous for ~**3 hours** (except very rough seas); also work on
  **snow** to write distress code letters.
- **Natural materials:** use high-contrast geometric patterns; in **snow** tramp out letters,
  in **sand** arrange boulders/vegetation/seaweed, in vegetation cut patterns.

---

## 7. Radio contact (if you have comms)

- **"Mayday, Mayday"**, then give: **call sign, your name, your location, number of
  survivors, available landing sites, and remarks** — so rescuers know exactly what they're
  flying into.
