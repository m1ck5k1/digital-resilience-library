# Navigation — Natural Navigation (Sun, Stars, Shadow)

> Category: **NAV** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary source: **US Army FM 21-76** (Survival) and **FM 3-25.26** — public-domain US
> Government field manuals. Verified 2026-09-07.
> **Not a substitute for a compass in terrain where one can be carried and checked.**

No compass? The sun, the moon, and the stars still give you direction — with patience and a
clear line of sight.

---

## 1. Finding east–west from the sun

- *"The sun always rises in the east and sets in the west, but not exactly due east or due
  west. There is also some seasonal variation."*
- Year-round relationship: the sun's noon position and shadow direction give you a true
  reading.

### Shadow-tip method (simplest)
1. Push a **~1 m straight stick** into level ground.
2. Mark the tip of its shadow. **The first mark is ALWAYS west, everywhere on earth.**
3. Wait **10–15 minutes** until the tip moves a few cm; mark it again.
4. The line through the two marks is **approx east–west**. Stand with the **first (west) mark
   on your left and the second on your right → you are facing north**.

### Shadow-clock (FM 3-25.26)
- The **west** part of the east–west line = **0600**; the **east** part = **1800**, anywhere on
  earth; the north–south line is the **noon line**. This makes every "day" 12 unequal hours
  and is **not intended above 60° latitude** (polar regions).

---

## 2. Watch method (using a wristwatch, daylight)

- **Northern hemisphere:** hold the watch horizontal, point the **hour hand at the sun**;
  bisect the angle between the hour hand and **12 o'clock** → that line is **north–south**.
  On **daylight-saving time**, use the midpoint between the hour hand and **1 o'clock**.
- **Southern hemisphere:** point **12** toward the sun; the midpoint between 12 and the hour
  hand gives the north–south line.
- Requires **true local time** to be accurate.

---

## 3. The moon

- If the moon **rises before sunset**, its illuminated side is **west**.
- If it **rises after midnight**, its illuminated side is **east**.

---

## 4. The North Star (Polaris) — Northern Hemisphere

- The **Big Dipper (Ursa Major)** and **Cassiopeia** are visible on clear nights and rotate
  **counterclockwise around Polaris**.
- **Big Dipper:** the two "pointer stars" (outer lip of the bucket) point to the North Star.
  Extend the line **about five times** the distance between the pointer stars → North Star.
- **Cassiopeia** ("W" of five stars): the North Star is straight out from its **center star**.

## 5. The Southern Cross (Crux) — Southern Hemisphere

- A tilted cross of stars; the **two stars of the long axis are the pointer stars**.
- Imagine a distance **five times their separation**; the point where that line ends is
  **south**.

---

## 6. What NOT to trust (natural myths)

- **Moss on trees is NOT reliable** — moss grows all around some trees; it's often more lush on
  the **south** side in the Northern Hemisphere, but it's not a dependable direction finder.
- **Tree growth rings** are more widely spaced on the **equator side** and closer together on
  the **pole side** — a weak indicator, not a substitute for the methods above.

---

## 7. Improvised compass (when you have no compass but have metal)

1. **Magnetise** a needle-shaped piece of ferrous metal or a razor blade by **stroking it in
   ONE direction** on silk or through hair — or make an **electromagnet** from a battery
   (min. 2 V) + an insulated wire coil.
2. Suspend it on a **non-metallic string**, or float it on wood/a leaf in water.
3. It will align to a **north–south line**.
