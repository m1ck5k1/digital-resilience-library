# Navigation — Map & Compass (Lost-Proofing)

> Category: **NAV** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary source: **US Army FM 3-25.26** (Map Reading and Land Navigation, 20 Jul 2001) and
> **FM 21-76** (Survival) — public-domain US Government field manuals. Verified 2026-09-07.
> **Not a substitute for current local guidance or hands-on practice with your own compass.**

The two halves of staying found: (1) know how to express and follow a **direction**, and
(2) know what to do the instant you suspect you're lost — **before** you wander further.

---

## 1. Direction: azimuths

- An **azimuth** is a horizontal angle measured **clockwise from a north base line** (true
  north, magnetic north, or grid north). It's the most common military way to express
  direction. A circle is **360 degrees** (or 6,400 mils).
- **Back azimuth** (the opposite direction):
  - Degrees: **add 180°** if the azimuth is ≤180°; **subtract 180°** if it's >180°.
  - Mils: add **3,200** if <3,200; subtract 3,200 if >3,200.
- A **magnetic azimuth** is the direction you read with magnetic instruments (lensatic, M2
  compass).

---

## 2. Declination (map vs compass north)

- Most larger maps carry a **declination diagram** in the lower margin showing the angular
  relationship (prongs) among **grid, magnetic, and true north**.
- The **G-M angle** is the angular size between **grid north and magnetic north**, expressed
  to the nearest **½ degree** (mils to nearest 10).
- Azimuths not adjusted for declination are **in error by the size of the declination angle**.
  Always apply the map's declination correction when converting between map and compass.

---

## 3. Compass limits & interference

- **Accuracy:** check your compass periodically on a known line of direction (e.g. a surveyed
  azimuth). **Do not use a compass that varies more than 3°.**

Suggested minimum separation distances from interference:
| Source of interference | Minimum distance |
|---|---|
| **High-tension power lines** | **55 m** |
| Field gun / truck / tank | 18 m |
| Telegraph / telephone / barbed wire | 10 m |
| Machine gun | 2 m |
| Steel helmet or rifle | ½ m |

---

## 4. Distance & pace count

- A **pace** = one natural step, about **30 inches (≈0.76 m)** long.
- **Find YOUR pace count** by walking a measured course of **100–600 m**; for a 600 m course,
  count total paces and divide by 6 to get paces per 100 m.
- Manual example: **120 paces to walk 100 m**; walking **up** a slope the count may rise to
  130 or more.
- **Track** distance with a physical aid — pebble moved per 100 m, knots in a string, or a
  notebook. **"Do not try to remember the count."**
- Pace varies with conditions: longer **downgrade**, shorter **upgrade**; head/tail wind;
  surface (sand, gravel, mud, snow); weather (snow/rain/ice); excess clothing; poor
  visibility.
- Conversions: **km→mi × 0.62**; mi→km ÷ 0.62 (i.e. **1 mi ≈ 1,600 m / 1.6 km**;
  **0.1 mi ≈ 160 m**).

> **Not sourced:** the common round figure "~2,000 steps ≈ 1 mile" was **not** found verbatim
> in these manuals. At the manual's 30-inch pace, a mile is ≈ 2,112 paces (an arithmetic
> derivation, not a stated figure). Use your own measured pace count to be safe.

---

## 5. The lost procedure — S U R V I V A L (think before you move)

From FM 21-76. **Before panicking and moving, run the acronym:**

- **S — Size up the situation.** Assess your surroundings, your physical condition (check for
  wounds, drink water, add clothing if cold/wet), and the equipment on hand.
- **U — Use all your senses; undue haste makes waste.** *"Don't move just for the sake of
  taking action… If you act in haste, you may… become disoriented so that you don't know
  which way to go."*
- **R — Remember where you are.** Spot your location on the map, relate it to the terrain,
  orient constantly, and note nearby water sources and cover/concealment.
- **V — Vanquish fear and panic** — your greatest enemies in a survival/evasion situation.
- **I — Improvise.** *"No matter how complete a survival kit you have with you, it will run
  out or wear out after a while."*
- **V — Value living.** The will to live / the refusal to give up.
- **A — Act like the natives.** Learn from locals/animals for food and water — but beware:
  animals may eat plants that are **toxic to humans**.
- **L — Live by your wits, but for now, learn basic skills.**

The survival **priority order** (FM 21-76): food, water, shelter, fire, first aid, and
signals "placed in order of importance" — **but if you're injured, first aid has top priority,
no matter the climate.**

> **Not sourced:** the "STOP — Stop, Think, Observe, Plan" mnemonic was **not** found in these
> manuals. The military **SURVIVAL** acronym above is the grounded framing to use.
