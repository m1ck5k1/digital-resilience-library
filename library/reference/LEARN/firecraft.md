# Skills & Trades — Firecraft (Building & Lighting a Fire)

> Category: **LEARN** · Part: **Reference** (public)
> Primary source: **US Army FM 21-76** (Survival) — public-domain US Government work.
> Verified 2026-09-08.
> **Not a substitute for local fire-safety / outdoor-guidance.** Practice before you need it,
> and follow current local fire rules.

Fire is warmth, light, safe water/food, and a signal. Building it well is a taught skill.

---

## 1. The fire triangle

*"The three sides of the triangle represent **air, heat, and fuel**. If you remove any of
these, the fire will go out."* Fuel in a non-gaseous state does not burn directly — heat
produces gas, which, *"combined with oxygen in the air, burns."*

---

## 2. Site selection & preparation

- Clear a circle **at least 1 m in diameter** so there is little chance of the fire spreading.
- Build a **fire wall** (logs or rocks) to reflect/direct heat, reduce flying sparks, and cut
  wind into the fire — but keep *"enough wind to keep the fire burning."*
- ⚠ **Do not use wet or porous rocks** — they may **explode when heated**.
- **Dakota fire hole:** dig a hole; on the upwind side, dig a large connecting ventilation hole;
  build the fire in the hole (conceals the fire, good for cooking).
- **In snow:** lay **green logs** side-by-side as a dry base, add 1–2 more layers, with the top
  layer laid opposite those below.

---

## 3. Materials — tinder, kindling, fuel

| Material | Definition (FM 21-76) |
|----------|------------------------|
| **Tinder** | Dry material that ignites with little heat — *"a spark starts a fire."* Must be **absolutely dry**. |
| **Kindling** | Readily combustible material added to burning tinder; absolutely dry; raises the temperature to ignite less-combustible material. |
| **Fuel** | Less-combustible material that burns slowly and steadily once ignited. |

- **Charred cloth** (heating cotton cloth until it turns black but does **not** burn) holds a
  spark for long periods. Keep it in an **airtight container** to stay dry; prepare it well in
  advance and add it to your kit.

---

## 4. Fire lays (how to build)

- **Tepee** — arrange tinder + a few kindling sticks in a cone; light the centre; as it burns,
  the outside logs fall inward to feed the fire. *"Burns well even with wet wood."*
- **Lean-to** — push a green stick into the ground at a **30° angle**, point-end into the wind;
  place tinder deep underneath; lean kindling against the stick; light the tinder.
- **Cross-ditch** — scratch a cross about **30 cm** across and **7.5 cm deep**; put a large wad
  of tinder in the centre; build a kindling pyramid above. The shallow ditch lets air sweep under
  the tinder to provide a draft.
- **Pyramid** — place two small logs parallel on the ground; lay a solid layer of smaller logs
  across; add 3–4 more layers, each smaller and at right angles to the layer below; make a starter
  fire on top. Burns downward, *"requiring no attention during the night."*

---

## 5. Lights (how to light) — always light from the **upwind** side

| Method | What the source says |
|--------|----------------------|
| **Matches** | Must be **waterproof**; store in a waterproof container with a reliable striker pad. |
| **Convex lens** | Only on bright, sunny days; angle the lens to concentrate the sun's rays on tinder; hold over one spot until it smoulders; blow/fan into a flame. |
| **Metal match** | Place a flat dry leaf under the tinder with part exposed; put the metal-match tip on the leaf; scrape a knife against it to produce sparks. |
| **Battery** | Attach a wire to each terminal; touch the bare wire ends together next to tinder so sparks ignite it. |
| **Gunpowder** | Extract the bullet from the casing carefully; *"a spark will ignite the powder"*; be **extremely careful**. |
| **Flint & steel** | *"Strike a flint or other hard, sharp-edged rock with a piece of carbon steel (**stainless steel will not produce a good spark**)."* Needs a loose wrist + practice. |
| **Fire-plow** (friction) | Rub a hardwood shaft against a softer wood base; cut a groove in the base and **plow** the blunt tip up and down it; the plowing pushes out wood-fibre particles; more pressure → friction ignites. |
| **Bow & drill** (friction) | Parts — **Socket** (stone/hardwood/bone with a slight depression), **Drill** (~**2 cm dia × 25 cm** seasoned hardwood, top rounded, low end blunt), **Fire board** (~**2.5 cm thick × 10 cm** seasoned softwood, with a depression ~2 cm from the edge and a V-cut under it), **Bow** (resilient green stick ~**2.5 cm dia** + string tied end-to-end). Loop the bowstring over the drill, press down with the socket, and saw the bow back and forth; grind the hot black powder into the tinder and blow until it ignites. |

> *"Primitive fire-building methods are exhaustive and require practice."*

**Helpful hints:** use **non-aromatic seasoned hardwood** for fuel; collect kindling/tinder
along the trail; keep firewood dry; **bank the fire** to keep coals alive overnight; **be sure
the fire is out** before leaving camp; don't pick wood lying on the ground (may look dry but
*"generally doesn't provide enough friction"*).

---

## 6. Safety & leaving the site
- Light from upwind; keep the fire contained in a cleared circle.
- **Never leave a fire unattended**; bank it to keep coals, and **fully extinguish** before
  leaving.
- Follow current local fire restrictions and guidance.
